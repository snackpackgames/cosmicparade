# Scoring is positional, continuous, and demand-driven

Tiles carry **no printed point value**. Points come from what a tile's *placement*
achieves against the viewer demands active in that pageant — a meld, a chain, a
burgeon stack. Score is continuous and visible per player; what stays secret is the
drafted objective, not the score.

Viewer demands are **domain-agnostic**: the audience wants *structures*, never a named
domain. This is deliberate and load-bearing — demanding a specific domain would lock
a player out of scoring entirely when their bag lacks it, which bites hardest early,
when a player holds only their starting kit's domain.

## Considered options

Printed base values multiplied by position were rejected: they invite "play the
biggest tile", turn crafting into a power ladder, and make the mosaic a stat race.
Domain-specific demands revealed before deck selection were rejected for the lockout
risk above, though they remain a candidate *bonus* layer once collections are wide.

## Consequences

A tile is not individually valuable, so player feedback must come from the running
score and the visible board state rather than from numbers printed on tiles.
