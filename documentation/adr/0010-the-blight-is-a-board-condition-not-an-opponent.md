# The blight is a board condition, not an AI opponent

In solo play the parade ground starts blighted and the rot spreads outward each round
by a fixed rule; the player's tiles cleanse and reclaim it. **No AI opponent is
written.**

An AI would reuse the whole multiplayer pageant system, which sounds cheap, but an AI
that plays a spatial mosaic deckbuilder competently with hidden objectives is among the
hardest things this design could ask for — and a weak one feels worse than none. A
spreading rule needs no AI, tunes through a single spread-rate number, and fits the
fiction of realms "polluted by neglect, pretermission, and abandonment" far better than
an adversary does.

## Consequences

Solo plays unlike multiplayer and needs its own tuning pass. Failing a cleansing means
withdrawing with a weak reward, not a game over — the blight presses without punishing,
which the cozy framing requires. Expect the "just add an AI opponent" suggestion to
recur; this is the reason it was declined.
